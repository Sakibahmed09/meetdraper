The rule that feeds the queue

No draft graveyard

There is no pile of half-finished posts sitting anywhere. When a piece is started, from a mined meeting or a voice note, Draper drives it to completion through the conversation and will not let you abandon it mid-way. Only finished, word-approved posts join the queue.

In progress

An open loop, not a draft

A piece being written is a live thread Draper is actively closing, chasing you for the missing line, the sharper opener, the yes. It is never filed away to rot.

In the queue

Only finished work

The moment a post is done, it takes a slot. The words are settled, so at its slot it simply goes. The autonomy ladder governs how the words get approved, not a second sign-off at publish.

How the system behaves

The rules, in one place

Timing is Draper's, words are yours. You never pick a date. Draper never writes the final word without your ladder-level permission.
Empty slot: nudge, never fill. If nothing is ready, Draper pings you to create. It never posts something you did not seed.
Arcs stay balanced. Draper spaces Founder, Builder and Pulse across slots so the feed adds up to something, and proposes the order for you to override.
Edit by talking. "Bump the fundraise post", "skip Thursday", "swap those two". No dashboard to learn, Draper does the mechanics.
Bumps are proposed, not imposed. Draper spots timely content and asks. The queue never reshuffles before you have seen it.
The cadence is sacred. Stacking and bumping change order and timing, never the number of posts a week you agreed to.

What we need from the Draper agent

The behaviour the scheduler has to deliver, grouped by responsibility.

Cadence
  • Read a posting frequency from plain language ("twice a week") and translate it into concrete slots, defaulting to proven days (2× → Tue/Thu, 3× → Mon/Wed/Fri).
  • Let the user lock specific days or times. Otherwise Draper owns them. Timing autonomy is full from day one.
Completion
  • Never accumulate half-finished drafts. Drive every started piece to done through the conversation and refuse to let it be abandoned mid-way.
  • Only completed, word-approved posts enter the queue.
Queue mechanics
  • Hold an ordered queue of finished posts mapped onto upcoming cadence slots. Slot zero is next to publish.
  • Stack: a newly finished post takes the next open slot at the back.
  • Balance the arcs across slots (no arc twice in a row); propose the order, allow override.
Bump (timely)
  • Detect time-sensitive content and propose a bump. Never reshuffle without a yes.
  • On confirm: the timely post takes the next slot, every other post shifts back exactly one, a new slot opens at the back. The cadence never inflates.
  • Keep "post right now" as a separate, rare manual override that breaks cadence and sits off-queue.
Publishing
  • At each slot, publish the front post automatically. The words were approved at completion; the timing is Draper's.
  • Govern the words by the autonomy ladder: approve-every → veto-window → autonomous completion.
Guardrails
  • Empty slot: nudge the user to create. Never fill a slot with unseeded content, never post what they did not start.
  • Editing is conversational: understand "bump X", "skip Thursday", "swap these", "move X after Y".
Loose ends

Proposed defaults, flag if wrong

The edges we did not decide out loud. Here is what I would ship unless you say otherwise.

Time of day
Draper uses a consistent default time per slot (say 8am local), the same each week for predictability, and can suggest a better time per post. Alternative: optimise the time individually every post.
Cadence change
Changing the frequency re-slots the existing queue onto the new rhythm, keeping the order. Alternative: new cadence applies only to future posts, existing slots stay put.
Horizon
No hard limit on how far you can stack, but Draper surfaces roughly the next three to four weeks so the queue stays legible.
