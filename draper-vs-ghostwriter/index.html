What you actually get from a human ghostwriter

The standard arrangement: a 60 to 90 minute onboarding interview, then recurring calls where the writer pulls stories out of you, turned into two or three posts a week. Mid-tier professionals charge $1,500 to $3,500 a month; senior writers and agencies run $3,500 to $6,000 and beyond, with premium executive programmes far higher. UK pricing is thinner on the ground; published entry points start around £299 a month.

When it works, it really works. The failure modes are equally consistent: the fee, turnaround measured in days, dependency on one writer's calendar, quality that varies enormously, and voice drift when your writer changes.

What running both teaches us

Because the agency is live, we see the same failure modes every month: the founder who cancels the story call three weeks running, the writer who nails the voice then leaves, the post that goes out because the slot was booked rather than because it was any good. Draper exists to fix those three specifically. Everything else the agency does well, a human still does better, and we say so below.

Where the human still wins

A senior ghostwriter who knows your industry brings relationships, taste formed over years, and the ability to push back on your strategy over dinner. If you have the budget and found one of the good ones, keep them. The honest problem is that most founders cannot tell the good ones from the confident ones until months of fees have gone.

Where Draper wins

Speed, consistency and the interview happening where you already are. Nothing to schedule: the extraction happens in your meetings and your texts. And the part we are proudest of carrying over from the agency: the willingness to say no. If the week produced nothing worth your name, Draper skips the slot, which is a discipline surprisingly few human arrangements survive.
